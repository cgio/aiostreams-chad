Stream Status (Cached/Uncached/Preloaded)

  • ► = Cached/playback-ready—ready to play—appears next to the service, e.g., 🟩 TB ▶.
  • ▻ = Cached/playback-ready, preload-selected—ready to play and pre-resolved by the server—appears next to the service, e.g., 🟩 TB ▷.
  • ▼ = Uncached/playback-delayed—not ready to play/cloud download required—appears next to the service, e.g., 🟩 TB ▼.
  • ▽ = Uncached/playback-delayed, actively pre-caching/downloading—presently downloading—appears next to the service, e.g., 🟩 TB ▽.

Proxy Status

  • 🅟 = Proxied—only appears when a stream will be proxied. Only seen by those with technical setups.

🎞️ Video Info (Quality/Source Material & Codec)

  • ORIGINAL = The top-shelf tier. Unique definition/attribute per this Formatter. Appears first in the video row to tell you at-a-glance that the stream comes from the best source possible (e.g., pristine Blu-Ray or untouched WEB-DL data).
  • COMMON = The everyday workhorse tier. Unique definition/attribute per this Formatter. Indicates standard, reliable mid-tier rips, compressed encodes, or HDTV files that look great without melting your bandwidth.
  • EARLY = The junk tier. Unique definition/attribute per this Formatter. Reserved for premature, unreleased leaks (e.g., CAM, TS, TC). Strictly for the impatient who don't mind a heavily compromised viewing experience.
  • REMUX = These streams/files are typically 60GB+ and should only be used by sufficiently capable devices and unlimited data internet plans. These are top-quality versions, come from the best source possible, and sometimes include additional languages, subtitles, etc.
  • BLU-RAY = The physical disc release, but unlike a heavy REMUX, these are standard compressed encodes (BDRips). They shrink the massive file size down to something reasonable while keeping high-fidelity visuals intact.
  • WEB = Sourced from original content, should be 1:1 like a REMUX, but the source is from an original internet (hence "WEB") movie file. WEB and other source material is self-explanatory, for the most part, except for the following elucidations. HEVC, AVC, and whatever else appears on the right side of the row are popular encoding formats and not likely to matter unless your device is allergic to the newer AV1 format.
  • WEB-RIP = Unlike pristine WEB (WEB-DL) files which are captured 1:1 from the streaming provider, a WEB-Rip is a re-encode. It's often captured by recording the stream or compressed afterward, trading a tiny bit of fidelity for a much lighter file.
  • HDTV = Sourced from a high-definition television broadcast. Great for catching network TV shows, but may occasionally come with a baked-in channel logo or slightly lower bitrates compared to a streaming release.
  • CAM = Camera captured—lowest-tier—traditional theater recording; may contain "bonus footage" of people taking their seats, shadow puppets, audible candy wrappers, and grunts across both the emotional and sound spectrums.
  • TS = Telesync—better than CAM—secondary theater projection record—utilizes an isolated direct audio connection line to strip theater environment noise channels out cleanly.
  • TC = Telecine—better than TS—direct analog cell transfer structure—pulls visual timing grids directly from archival 35mm film components before processing digital formats.
  • Remember, you can always ask your friendly, neighborhood AIOStreams dealer to exclude early/unreleased/"unofficial" files, which withholds these premature rips of lesser quality from arousing your temptations. If you want more terminology details, check out the official TRaSH Guides Quality Profiles.

Further Customization

Change as you please. The concept is as follows.

  • Don't want to see "release" info that appears in the last row? Use your wits and delete that portion but be sure not to delete the message portion.
  • Don't want to see HEVC/AVC, etc. due to little variety and relevance to the layman viewer? Delete the relevant portion. Hint: {stream.encode}.

Notes

  • The AIOStreams Formatter is limited. Pleasing results require workarounds. For example, {streams.editions} is an array, yet four booleans ({stream.upscaled}, {stream.regraded}, {stream.uncensored}, {stream.unrated}) exist for qualities of a similar nature. Consequentially, the join() array modifier (used for cleanly display array contents with non-orphaned separators), cannot be used on these booleans the same way as {streams.editions}. Therefore, careful workarounds (as I have done) are required.
  • A popular streaming app provides two columns with only so many rows, each of the same limited length for media information display. Truncation occurs in excess. Any good Formatter template will attempt to maximize displayed media information without truncation. As such, when duration/{stream.duration} does not exist, this affords an extra row that is used to display those edition and boolean qualifiers, should they exist, and if so, they appear on their own row prefixed with ⭐️. Conversely, should duration/{stream.duration} exist as well as one or more of those qualifiers, the qualifiers are displayed to the right of the duration text after a | (pipe), rather than a ⭐️ (as to preserve my rule that emojis are to be limited to the first character of each row except for the bottom-most "release info" row). Why? Arguably, the qualifier information is somewhat negligible to the average viewer. Also, multiple qualifiers for the same media rarely exist in the wild. Secondly, preserving an extra row where possible is preferred. Thirdly, the duration text is always relatively short and affords room to the right. You may infer this is also why abbreviations are used for the various qualifiers—there is the possibility many may apply to certain media, even if exceedingly rare.
